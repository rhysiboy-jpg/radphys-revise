    Try this

    1. Drop the kV below 70. The sharp lines vanish: electrons no longer carry the 69.5 keV needed to eject a tungsten K-shell electron.
    2. Pin, then double the mA. Same shape and same maximum energy, twice the photons.
    3. Pin, then add filtration. The low-energy end is cut away, the mean energy rises and there are fewer photons overall. This is beam hardening.

    For Rhys: what to check

    • Physics: part descriptions, step narration, and the spectrum trends (line energies 59 and 67 keV, K-shell binding energy 69.5 keV, mean energy roughly half the kV).
    • Level: is the detail right for first years, too much or too little? Anything you'd add, such as the line-focus principle or the anode heel effect?
    • Wording: does it match how you teach it and Ball & Moores' terms?
    • Look and feel: diagram style, colours (electrons blue, X-rays magenta, heat orange) and layout on your phone.
